💵 Why Paying Cash Is Good for Everyone When You Thrift

Guide October 13, 2026 · 3 min read

A few folded bills in your pocket can make a thrift day a little better for everyone: the shop, the shopper and, at charity shops, the cause the shop supports. Here is why cash is worth carrying, and why cards are still welcome too.

Every card swipe costs the shop

When you pay by card, the card company charges the shop a fee, often around 3% of the sale. On a $10 find that is about 30 cents. It does not sound like much, but a thrift shop or a booth sells a lot of small items, and those cents add up quickly. At a charity shop, they are dollars that could have gone to the mission. For a vendor running a small business, they come out of an already thin margin. You can read more in what it costs to be a vendor.

Why some shops set a card minimum

Because fees add up on small sales, some shops ask for a minimum before they take a card, often $5 or $10. Others add a small card fee at the register. Neither is meant to be unfriendly. It is a way to keep the doors open. Where a manager has told us, the Trail shows the card minimum and the payment options right on the shop’s card, on the line that starts with “Pay.”

Cash helps you, too

  • It keeps you on budget. When the bills in your pocket are gone, you are done. That makes it easier to stay under your limit.
  • Checkout is quicker. Handing over bills is fast, and it keeps the line moving on a busy Saturday.
  • No surprises. You will not run into a card minimum or a card fee when you pay cash.
  • It works when the internet does not. A card reader needs a connection. Cash does not.

A simple cash plan for the Trail

  • Stop at your bank’s ATM before you start, so you are not paying a machine fee later.
  • Ask for small bills. Ones, fives and tens make it easy to pay exactly.
  • Keep a card tucked away for the big find. If you fall in love with a piece, you can still take it home.
  • Before you go, glance at the “Pay” line on each shop’s card on the Trail so you know who takes what.

Cards are fine, too

Shops accept cards because many of us do not carry cash, and a sale on a card is much better than no sale at all. If paying by card is easier for you, go ahead. The point is simply that a little cash goes a long way, and the shops notice. Whether you are digging through a bin at a charity shop or browsing a carefully styled booth, we’re all thrift and vintage enthusiasts.
