🎒 Back-to-School Thrifting: Clothes, Backpacks and Supplies for Less

Seasonal August 2, 2027 · 2 min read

Back-to-school spending adds up fast. Kids outgrow clothes in months, and everything they need seems to go on sale at the same moment as everyone else's list. Thrift stores can take a big bite out of the bill, and kids' clothing is some of the best secondhand value around.

What to thrift

  • Jeans and pants. Check knees and hems for wear.
  • Jackets and sweatshirts. Look for working zippers and no stains.
  • Backpacks and lunchboxes. Check the seams and zippers. Wash before use.
  • Shoes. Look at the soles and insoles. Thrifted shoes should have plenty of tread left.
  • Books and supplies. Binders, folders, calculators and library-quality books.

What to buy new

Underwear, socks, sneakers for sports and anything that needs to fit exactly are usually worth buying new. Everything else is fair game.

Where to go

The Thrift Store benefiting Chambliss Center on Brainerd Rd is known for a good plus-size and kids selection. Larger stores such as Goodwill and America's Thrift Stores have large children's sections.

Make it fun

Let kids pick one or two items that are totally theirs. A vintage jacket or a graphic tee they chose gives them a sense of style and ownership.

Plan before you shop

Check the school's dress code and supply list first, and measure kids' current sizes. Take a list, but expect to find a few surprises. Wash everything before it goes in a backpack.

Quick answers

Is it worth thrifting school clothes?

Yes. Kids outgrow things quickly, and secondhand jeans, jackets and sweaters can last through several wearers.

What should I buy new?

Underwear, socks, shoes with specific support needs and anything with safety standards.

Thrift tip: Shop a month before school starts. Selection is best and you will have time to wash and mend.
