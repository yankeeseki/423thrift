🧥 How to Spot Quality Clothing at the Thrift Store

Tips September 6, 2027 · 2 min read

A thrift store rack can look like a wall of sameness. The skill that changes everything is learning to spot quality quickly, so you can flip through a hundred items in a few minutes and pull the five worth trying on.

Start with the fabric

Check the tag first. Wool, cashmere, silk, linen, leather and heavy cotton usually hold up better and look better than thin polyester blends. Read the content label before you read the brand.

Feel the weight

Quality garments usually feel substantial. A good shirt has body when you hold it, and a good coat has some heft. Thin, see-through or scratchy fabrics are a sign to move on.

Look at the stitching

  • Seams should be straight and even with small, tight stitches.
  • Patterns should line up at seams and pockets.
  • Buttons should be sewn on firmly, and buttonholes should be neat.
  • Check linings for tears and make sure zippers run smoothly.

Check for problems

Look under the arms, at the collar, along the hem and at the knees. Hold the piece up to the light to see thin spots. Pilling, fading and stains are okay to buy if the price is right and you know you can fix them, but be honest with yourself.

Try everything on

Sizing varies by decade and brand. Try it on whenever you can. If you cannot, hold it against your body, measure the shoulders and check the length.

Brands matter less than construction

A well-made garment from an unfamiliar label often beats a trendy brand that is poorly sewn. Check how it is built, not just who made it.

Quick answers

Which fabrics last longest?

Wool, cotton, linen, silk and leather generally last longer than thin synthetics.

Should I try everything on?

Yes. Sizes vary widely across decades and brands.

Thrift tip: Make a first pass that is just for color and fabric. Pull things that catch your eye, then inspect them closely. You will move faster and find more.

Charity thrift stores are great for volume and variety, and smaller shops such as Northside Neighborhood House on the North Shore are known for higher-end donated brands at reasonable prices.
